Same Script, Two AI Video Tools: Fliki Exported While Pictory Was Still Rendering

A 792-character script, two free tiers, one afternoon, and the real numbers behind both exports.

I publish a short video every weekday morning for a small channel about work tools, and once a week I want a 60-second explainer that looks like someone made it on purpose. I don't edit video. So the question I keep coming back to is embarrassingly simple: if you paste the same script into two "script-to-video" tools, how far apart are they really?

This week I found out. Same script, same afternoon, free tiers only, no card. Here is what happened, including the parts that were worse than the demo reels.

Disclosure first, because it belongs at the top: some links in this post are affiliate links. If you sign up through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Both tools were tested the same way regardless, and every price below was read on the vendor's own pages or inside the app on 27 November 2026.

The test

  • One script, 792 characters, about the UK's 2022 four-day-week trial. At narration pace it comes out around 54 seconds.
  • Fliki: free plan, 16:9, English voice "Jonah" (clear/professional), one scene per sentence, auto visuals, subtitles on.
  • Pictory: free trial, text-to-video, default AI voice ("Tom"), auto visuals, one scene per sentence, "highlight keywords with AI" on.
  • Both from the same Korean IP, same browser, one afternoon. No credit card entered for either.

What actually happened

Fliki took about two minutes to turn the script into 11 scenes; Pictory took about five just to "craft" its 11 scenes, then a few more minutes to render. Both timelines ran to roughly the same length (48.5 seconds and 50.6 seconds), and both exported at 16:9. After that, they stop resembling each other.

On the free tiers, Fliki exports a 720p MP4 (1280×720, 5.9 MB, 48.5 seconds) with its watermark. Pictory's trial export also carries its branding ("Created with PICTORY"), but the file it gave me was 1920×1080, 10.6 MB, 56.8 seconds long — longer than my timeline, because it added a title card and an end card of its own.

Fliki: fast, fine-grained, and it cuts sentences in the wrong place

Fliki returned an editable timeline in about two minutes, which I did not expect. What comes back is a deck-shaped video: 11 scenes, each with a stock clip, a large auto-written headline ("4-DAY WEEK", "32 HOURS", "HIDDEN DETAIL", "MEETINGS GONE"), the narration line underneath, and subtitles burned in.

What earned its keep:

  • Every scene is editable in place — swap the footage, change the duration, edit the on-screen text, and download that one scene's voiceover as audio.
  • Keyword highlighting on the spoken line, which makes a 50-second clip survivable without a motion designer.
  • A voice picker that actually matters. The free plan lists 300 voices across 80+ languages. One trap: the default was "auto voice, Korean" because of the account locale. If you don't change it, a Korean voice reads your English script.

What annoyed me:

  • Sentence splitting. My longest sentence got cut mid-clause across two scenes: scene 6 ended on "...the work did not get shorter because" and scene 7 restarted from "people worked faster." Captions and narration then drift for about six seconds. You can fix it by hand, but the real fix is writing shorter sentences.
  • Template sameness. Several scenes reused near-identical office footage. For a channel that posts daily, that is the tell that a machine made it.
  • The free plan is one test. 3 credits a month, which the pricing FAQ calls about five minutes of finished video.

Pictory: slower, better paced, and quietly adding things

Pictory spent roughly five minutes just "crafting the scenes," then a few more rendering. By the time it hit 70%, Fliki's MP4 was already in my downloads folder.

But the output reads differently. Pictory sets each scene's duration from the voice track — 3.79 s, 6.43 s, 6.82 s, 2.04 s — so cuts land with the sentence instead of around it, and the captions follow the same breaks. My script's longest sentence survived in one piece.

Then the surprises, in the order I noticed them:

  • It wrote a title card I never asked for — "A bold workplace experiment that changed how the world thinks about time" over a stock shot of a paper reading WORK WEEK — plus an end card. My 50.6-second timeline came out as a 56.8-second file.
  • The trial's resolution cap was not enforced on my export. The pricing page says the trial is 720p; the file Pictory handed me is 1920×1080, with a "Created with PICTORY" mark in the corner.
  • It is built to be driven from an AI assistant. The MCP server is listed as included on every plan, with Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Cursor and VS Code named on the page. I did not exercise that flow here — that is what the pricing page lists, not what I confirmed. For a developer, it is the more interesting half of the product.
  • The trial is genuinely a trial. 14 days, 300 credits ("up to 10 minutes"), 5-minute maximum video length, 3 videos. My account's trial had already expired from a past look and the app offered a one-click 14-day extension — convenient for testing, but don't count on that being endless.

What it costs when the free test ends

  • Fliki: free is $0 with 3 credits a month, 720p and a watermark. Standard is $28 per user per month (annual billing halves it): 180 credits a month, 1080p, no watermark, videos up to 15 minutes, 1,000 voices.
  • Pictory: the free tier is the 14-day trial (300 credits, 3 videos, 5-minute cap, watermarked). Starter is $12.50 a month billed annually, down from a $29 list price, during a Black Friday offer ending 28 November 2026; that buys 24,000 credits a year, up to 360 minutes.

Cost per finished minute, which is the number I actually care about:

  • Pictory Starter: about $150 a year for 360 minutes — roughly $0.42 per finished minute.
  • Fliki Standard: $336 a year for 180 credits a month, which Fliki's own planning screen translates to about 150 minutes of stock-footage video a month, or about 45 minutes of AI video a month — roughly $0.08/min for stock-based video, $0.62/min for AI video.

Neither price is outrageous. Both are priced for people who publish every week, which is exactly why the free tier has to be judged on how much of your real workflow it lets you finish.

What I'd actually pay for

  • If you publish often and want to change things scene by scene, Fliki. The editor is the product, and two minutes to a first cut is real.
  • If you want one well-paced explainer a week and you already live in Claude or Cursor, Pictory, for the pacing and the MCP path — just budget for the auto title card and check the 5-minute trial cap before you plan a longer video.
  • If you need one video a quarter, neither. Both free tiers will produce one usable test video. Make it your real script, not a sample.

And the warning that applies to both: neither output was publish-ready. I spent about ten minutes per video fixing a split sentence, one caption that overflowed its box, and two pieces of stock footage that said "generic office" louder than the narration did. Any review that tells you these tools replace the edit is selling something.
